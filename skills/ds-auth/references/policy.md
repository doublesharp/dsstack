# Authentication policy

These are project defaults and design requirements, not a claim of NIST certification. Apply profiles per capability: a product can combine ordinary customer access with a privileged operator plane. The user and the application's explicit contract control exceptions.

## Choose a profile

| Profile | Default | Recovery and sensitive actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local, no online identity | No account for features that stay on the device. Use OS permissions and a keystore access policy matched to each secret; require fresh OS verification for sensitive unlock or export. | Back up encrypted data and its recovery key separately. Account recovery cannot recreate a lost decryption key. |
| Ordinary online account | Passkey-first; allow synced passkeys and FIDO2 keys with user verification. Verify email when used for contact or recovery. | Offer an independent backup credential and saved recovery codes. Require fresh verification with the strongest enrolled method allowed by the action policy. During migration, password plus TOTP may authorize first-passkey enrollment. |
| Privileged control plane | Require PIN/biometric-protected, non-exportable hardware credentials and two independently enrolled keys before activating privileges. | Use an existing enrolled key to replace a lost one. All-key loss uses independently authenticated operator approval or a pre-established infrastructure trust root, with audit and notification. Email, TOTP, or a consumer social login cannot bypass this profile. |

Privileged control-plane capabilities include changing authentication/recovery policy, granting privileged roles, administering production infrastructure, changing trusted software publishers, or controlling high-value fund-signing authority. Ordinary editing, routine billing, and a role named "admin" do not automatically require this profile. Specify the actions and trust boundary, including separation of app login from wallet/contract signing authority.

## Factors and enrollment

- Use WebAuthn/FIDO2 through the established provider or maintained library. Require user verification at registration and authentication and verify it on the server. Biometrics/PIN activate the authenticator locally; collect neither. A verified passkey ceremony normally needs no additional TOTP prompt.
- For new passwordless credentials, require discoverable credentials, offer conditional browser UI where supported, and accept platform and roaming authenticators. Keep an explicit sign-in button and browser-assisted cross-device option. Credential availability differs by device and client; preserve an accessible supported alternative.
- Fix a stable, narrowly scoped RP ID and exact allowed origins before enrollment. Keep staging and production credentials separate. Use opaque stable user handles. Persist, bind, expire, and atomically consume challenges, including failed finish attempts, so replay cannot create authority.
- Store public keys, credential IDs, verification-relevant properties, and counter data through the library. Support authenticators whose signature counter is zero or behaves differently under sync; apply the library's documented counter policy. Record backup eligibility/state without treating it as proof of hardware protection. For hardware-only capabilities, reject backup-eligible credentials and establish hardware provenance through a verified attestation chain, approved authenticator models, and trusted metadata, or through an audited provisioning procedure that binds the actual inventoried key to its enrollment. Record whether the assurance is cryptographic or operational. An AAGUID, attachment hint, or "security key" label alone is insufficient; fail closed when required evidence is unavailable.
- Require fresh authentication at the strength needed by the capability before adding/removing factors, changing recovery addresses, or linking identities, except for the bounded compromise-revocation rule below. Bootstrap enrollment is a separate flow bound to its account, intended recipient, browser/session where applicable, purpose, and expiry. Email bootstrap may establish a new ordinary account with full ordinary trust under the selected signup policy; recovery restrictions apply to existing accounts. Bootstrap must not overwrite an existing account's trusted credentials or mint privileged access.
- Encourage independent backups. Two synced copies of the same credential share a trust dependency. Privileged backup keys must be distinct credentials stored separately; avoid shared operator accounts. Prevent accidental removal of the last usable method while permitting authenticated compromise revocation. Without fresh verification, an existing session may revoke only the credential that established it. Revoking any other credential requires fresh verification with a remaining factor, recovery proof, or the configured trust root. Revocation ends sessions and delegated grants established by that credential, including the requesting session where applicable, and notifies pre-existing channels. An unverified report cannot revoke credentials. If none remain, enter recovery. Suspend privileged capabilities below the two-key requirement; a remaining approved key may authorize replacement enrollment as a maintenance-only operation. Preserve a recoverable privileged operator path.

## Passwords, TOTP, email, and external identities

Passkeys are the default for new online authentication. Existing passwords can remain during migration or for a documented compatibility need. If used, allow password managers and paste, block known compromised/common choices, use an established memory-hard password hasher with bounded concurrency, and avoid composition rules or routine forced rotation.

TOTP is a compatibility factor, commonly paired with passwords. Encrypt its verifier secret, confirm enrollment, throttle attempts, and consume accepted time steps once. TOTP and typed recovery codes are phishable. A saved code used alongside a password as a backup second factor is authentication, not automatically account recovery. Record the method in session/step-up state, apply the action policy, notify on backup-code use, and offer factor replacement. An authenticator app may hold either TOTP secrets or passkeys; describe the actual method.

Email links/codes may bootstrap or serve a deliberately lower-assurance ordinary-account path. Rate-limit, expire, bind to the intended flow, and atomically consume them. Require explicit confirmation before consumption where mail scanners could follow links. Prevent login CSRF and open redirects. Choose and document whether cross-device redemption is supported. SMS and security questions are not standard fallback choices.

Use OIDC authorization code with PKCE, state, nonce, and exact issuer/audience/redirect validation for federated login. Provider API authorization is a separate grant from application identity. Identify an external account by issuer/provider and immutable subject, not matching email or domain. Linking an existing identity requires proof of control and fresh verification; resolve account collisions explicitly. Accept external authentication strength only through claims and policies whose semantics the app has verified. Verify issuer-specific `auth_time`, `acr`/`amr`, and reauthentication semantics (`max_age` or `prompt=login` where supported). A refreshed token or a new login through an existing SSO cookie is not proof of fresh strong verification.

## Recovery and restoration of trust

Prefer another enrolled credential over recovery. For ordinary accounts with all credentials lost, use a saved recovery code plus a previously verified recovery channel as the fast path. Before launch, choose and document an all-proof-loss outcome: a pre-established independent recovery arrangement, an explicitly enabled delayed mailbox-recovery path, or disclosed unrecoverability. Do not leave a restricted session with no defined completion or rejection outcome. Generate at least 128 random bits per saved code, display once, store hashes, throttle, and consume atomically. Allow replacement sets after appropriate fresh verification and invalidate the old set.

Specify recovery as a state machine: requested, proofs satisfied, restricted session, credential enrollment, independent promotion, complete or canceled. Track the recovery flow and account security generation so concurrent or stale ceremonies cannot resurrect revoked authority. A recovery session and credentials enrolled through it retain their restriction even after a successful WebAuthn assertion. Existing pre-recovery sessions receive an in-app notice and may cancel the flow. A pre-existing enrolled credential or a purpose-bound cancellation token delivered to a pre-existing verified channel can also cancel it; cancellation invalidates only that flow's restricted sessions, pending ceremonies, and newly enrolled credentials. A cancellation token cannot grant access or revoke unrelated original credentials.

Define a reachable promotion rule for each supported recovery path. Saved code plus separate channel proof may restore ordinary access after new credential verification. A product that accepts mailbox control as its ordinary-account recovery trust root may instead enable delayed email-only promotion (starting choice: 72 hours), notify all available pre-existing verified channels, and provide cancellation throughout the delay. Until promotion, the delayed-recovery session may only enroll replacement credentials, view its recovery status, and cancel; it receives no account data or ordinary capabilities. Time adds an objection window, not a second proof. If ordinary email login remains available, a delay on recovery alone does not protect that same data; choose a consistent access policy. Email-only promotion retains its provenance and restores only ordinary capabilities; it cannot satisfy actions requiring independent recovery proof or restore privileged capabilities. Define how each withheld capability can be restored through independent proof, or disclose that it remains unavailable. If that trade-off is unacceptable, require the independent arrangement or disclose the loss condition during setup.

Privileged recovery uses independently authenticated operator approval grounded in a pre-established arrangement. For first-operator bootstrap and single-owner all-key-loss recovery, an infrastructure trust root independent of the lost web account can serve instead: for example, a protected local provisioner requiring host/database authority, or a separately held sealed recovery key. Establish the procedure before the incident, bind replacement enrollment to it, and require audit and notifications. Require a delay for all-key-loss restoration; choose a second approval for high-impact multi-operator deployments. Bootstrap provisions the first two keys before activating privileges. Email control or the lost web session alone cannot invoke this root. A new account with no prior proofing cannot be recovered by inventing identity questions.

Notify pre-existing verified channels of recovery and factor changes. An unauthenticated recovery request sends bounded notices and never revokes account authority. At recovery completion/promotion, revoke old browser sessions, outstanding enrollment/reset flows, and delegated grants according to the documented compromise boundary. On verified compromise proof, the contract may suspend affected authority earlier; preserve cancellation and the independent recovery path. Retire superseded or compromised authenticators explicitly at completion so old credentials cannot restore the previous authority. Recheck independent upstream tokens and signing keys separately; local session revocation does not revoke them. Rotate remaining saved recovery material on completion. Log the actor, affected account, action, and result without credentials or raw recovery links.

For encrypted local data, use the vault's supported key wrapping, OS keystore, and tested offline recovery. A passkey assertion authenticates; it is not itself a reusable encryption key. Any PRF/hmac-secret key-wrapping design needs explicit platform support and an independent recovery path.

## Sessions and application permissions

Prefer opaque random browser session credentials in Secure, HttpOnly, host-only cookies, using the `__Host-` prefix where supported. Store verifier hashes and server-enforced expiry/revocation; use an equivalent revocation design if the existing framework uses signed sessions. Choose SameSite for the actual login flow, check origins, protect cookie-authenticated mutations against CSRF, and mark auth responses non-cacheable. Keep long-lived credentials out of browser JavaScript storage and URLs.

Starting limits: ordinary remembered login up to 30 days; privileged active sessions up to 12 hours with 15–30 minutes inactivity; sensitive-action verification no older than five minutes. Applications should choose shorter limits or a different documented usability trade-off where needed. Remembered access is not five-minute-fresh authentication. Refresh and background requests must not silently extend absolute lifetime or strong-verification time.

Record subject, session/credential ID, authentication method, actual verification time, enrollment trust/recovery state, and security generation. Server verification creates these properties. Rotate sessions at login and assurance changes. Revocation and account disable must be effective on subsequent protected operations; if access tokens remain valid, bound and document that window and provide immediate checks for sensitive operations.

Resolve current membership, ownership, role, resource permissions, and required authentication strength at the authoritative operation. Recheck at commit for sensitive mutations. Authentication alone grants no tenant membership, publishing right, wallet authority, or ability to expand one's grant. Keep human, delegated-agent, and service principals distinct.

## CLI, MCP, native clients, and provider connections

Humans approve client grants in a trusted browser or a supported device-authorization flow. Use authorization code + PKCE for public clients, audience/resource-bound access tokens, bounded scope/resource consent, revocation, and short-lived access tokens. Rotate refresh tokens with replay/family revocation or use appropriate sender constraints. Authenticate and authorize every transport through the same application operation; a CLI/MCP adapter is not an admin shortcut. Grant-approval time cannot satisfy human freshness for later actions. Exclude human-step-up operations from delegable scopes or require a one-use trusted-browser approval bound to the exact actor, action, resource, parameters, and expiry.

Native clients use the system browser or platform credential APIs and protected credential storage; verify origin/app associations where needed. Automated workers use separately scoped service identities and cannot perform interactive human step-up or mint broader grants. Upstream provider tokens remain encrypted server-side, separately revocable, and distinct from the app's sessions. Keep independent apps' cookies, keys, and databases separate unless federation is explicitly selected.

## Security settings and acceptance

Provide clear sign-in choices, passkey names and last-use information, credential removal, backup/recovery setup, active sessions, delegated clients with scopes, and revocation controls. Explain canceled prompts, missing credentials, expired links, and cross-device choices without exposing whether a stranger's account exists. Keep recovery restrictions visible to the signed-in user.

Check the affected lifecycle end to end: supported device enrollment/login, another credential, last-factor removal, expired/replayed ceremonies, recovery completion/cancellation, independent promotion, session/grant revocation, tenant isolation, and current authorization. Use actual browsers/devices for claims about platform behavior. Test server rules with fixtures; do not claim device acceptance from mocked authenticators. Record the chosen profile and any remaining deployment/device checks in the application's existing architecture or task record.

- [WebAuthn](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/): server verification, origin/RP binding, user verification, backup properties, and attestation.
- [NIST authenticators](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/), [assurance](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal/), and [lifecycle/recovery](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/events/): factor properties, non-exportable keys, recovery proofs, and notifications.
- [OWASP MFA](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Multifactor_Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html) and [sessions](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html): factor replacement, session lifecycle, and revocation.
- [OAuth security BCP](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9700.html) and [MCP authorization](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/authorization): PKCE, refresh replay, and audience/resource binding.

The profiles, time limits, and rollout choices above are author-selected defaults informed by those sources. They are not universal requirements from the cited standards.
