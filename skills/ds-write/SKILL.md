# Write for the reader

Identify reader, purpose, format, and voice from the request and surrounding text. Use Oxford commas in lists of three or more items unless the user specifies another style. Match a supplied writing sample's vocabulary, sentence length, and rhythm. Draft from supported sources. In a light edit, leave clear passages as written.

- Lead with what the reader came to learn or do. Describe what something does in familiar words and supported specifics. Keep the steps for one task together. Cut empty framing, praise, repeated conclusions, and filler sections.
- Put a qualification beside the claim it limits. Keep it when deleting it would change the meaning, confidence, or the reader's next action.
- Write general docs and posts around supported behavior and normal use so they stay useful across releases. Cut incidental rollout history, staging details, and freshness stamps. Keep dates, versions, and commits when they establish compatibility or deadlines, anchor a dated narrative, or let the reader verify or reproduce a result.
- Inspect paragraphs for repeated openings, forced groups, uniform sentence shapes, and habitual em dashes; replace habitual dashes outside quotes with ordinary punctuation or a new sentence. Keep useful formatting, humor, established terms, and real three-item lists.
- Preserve facts, quantities, conditions, uncertainty, attribution, the author's position, code, commands, identifiers, paths, link targets, metadata, and exact quotations. Cutting a hedge must not strengthen the claim. Never invent facts, citations, anecdotes, opinions, or deliberate errors.

For a tool that stores client comments and approval history, write directly: "Review client comments and see who approved each revision."

Revise once and check accuracy, meaning, and voice against the source. Try deleting each disclaimer, contrast (even across sentences), and sentence that restates or interprets the previous point. Keep it only if it passes the qualification test above.

Return finished text, or edit the file and summarize briefly. Give a critique only when asked; an audit-only request leaves the text unchanged.
